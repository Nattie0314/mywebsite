Elevated Leads

Getting into the technical side of SEO

In 2025, I joined Elevated Leads as a Product Manager, later progressing into a broader leadership role covering SEO, product and growth.

One of the biggest challenges was working on CryptoCasinos.com after a Google core update had significantly affected the site's search visibility.

There wasn't one simple fix.

We had to look at keyword targeting, website structure, indexing problems, internal linking and how the content was organised. I also worked closely with a developer on migrating the website from a custom CMS to headless WordPress and Next.js, including URL structures, redirects and post-launch SEO checks.

Over the following months, the site's average Google search position improved from 47.6 in February to 19.3 in August 2026.

Between April and August, Google Search Console also recorded a 197% increase in organic clicks and a 344% increase in impressions.

Those improvements came from coordinated work across SEO, content and product, not one isolated change.

For me, the most interesting part was understanding how the different problems connected, deciding what needed attention first and seeing the website gradually recover.

How I approach SEO

I don't believe every website needs more content, more backlinks or a complete redesign.

Sometimes the problem is technical. Sometimes the content is targeting the wrong searches. And sometimes a website already has valuable opportunities sitting just outside Google's first page.

I prefer to understand what's happening before recommending what to do.

A few things matter to me:

  • Looking beyond the numbers.

    More traffic or impressions don't always mean better performance. I want to understand what's actually changed and why.

  • Finding the underlying problem.

    If pages aren't getting indexed or rankings are dropping, I look for the cause rather than repeatedly treating the symptoms.

  • Understanding search intent.

    Keyword tools are useful, but understanding what someone really wants when they search is just as important.

  • Being straightforward about the results.

    If something hasn't worked, I'll say so. The point of reporting is to help make better decisions, not just show charts that look impressive.

  • Making recommendations practical.

    You should understand what needs fixing, why it matters and where to start.

That's the approach I bring to NS Visibility.
